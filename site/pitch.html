We are not proposing a better AI reviewer. We are proposing a way to delegate review authority to AI that a named person can still sign for.

2 · The question is wrong

Stop asking "did a human read every line?" Ask "did a named human sign for what was proven, what was refused, and the risk that is left?"

Agent output outgrew line-by-line review. "The AI said it was fine" is not acceptable either. So the unit of review becomes a signed statement about evidence, not a read diff.

3 · The concept: authority is written down

A signed mandate per repo and area says what the AI may clear alone, and everything else goes to a named owner with an evidence pack.

factsindependent reviewerscitation checkdecideevidence packowner signsledgertrust per area
✓ SILENT✓ NOTIFYAI may clear, if the mandate allows? ASK✗ BLOCKa named human
Facts come first, so models argue about evidence instead of inventing it. A clause is proven only when every reviewer agrees with verified evidence. Anything else is refused with a reason, and a refusal is an output, not a failure.

7 · What we saw go wrong

Evidence made one reviewer agree with everything; the rules are built so one reviewer's "all good" can't clear anything.

  • Given passing tests, Codex's second pass marked all 11 clauses met with no findings; Claude's second pass found a remaining crash.
  • A requirement can be met before the change, so "met" isn't "achieved".
  • 228 of 2297's 237 files are generated; size rules that count them measure the generator.
Unanimity means every reviewer we add can only make the AI more cautious. The adjudicator can explain a dispute but the engine forbids it to clear anything.

8 · Limits, and the three questions

We know where this stops working, and the pack says so every time.

  • Limits: can't judge intent; only as good as the requirement; areas without checks fall back to humans; reviewers share training data; trust numbers here are simulated.
  • Judges' questions: Not trust our output? citations stop verifying, reviewers always agree, seeded defects pass, any escape. Who owns it? the owner of the most sensitive area, named in the signed mandate. Scale? humans see only what can't be proven; open-weight reviewers for confidential code.
Close on the punchline: it clears what it can prove, refuses what it can't, and a named human countersigns the rest.
